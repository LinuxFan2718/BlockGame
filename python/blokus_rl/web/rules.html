THE CORNER GAME

A simple rule.
A whole board of possibilities.

You and the bot each have 21 different pieces. Take turns placing one piece on the 14×14 board. The aim is to get as many of your squares onto the board as possible.

  1. Start on a star. Your first piece must cover one of the two marked starting squares. The other player starts on the other square.
  2. Meet at the corners. Every later piece must touch at least one of your own pieces corner to corner. Your own pieces must never share an edge. You may touch the opponent along edges or corners.
  3. Rotate, flip, place. Click a piece in your tray. Use the buttons or R to rotate and F to flip. Point at the board to preview; click when the preview is legal. The pointed square anchors the top-left corner of the shape’s bounding box. Escape clears your selection.
  4. No overlap or overhang. Every square of your piece must fit on the board and be empty. “Show legal spots” marks possible anchor squares for the selected orientation.
  5. Passes are automatic. If a player has no legal placement, the app passes for them. The game ends after both players have no placement.

Scoring

Your score begins at −89, the total area of your pieces. Each square you place removes one penalty point. Place all your pieces for a +15 bonus, with another +5 if the single square was your final piece. The player with the higher final score wins; equal scores draw.

Playing locally

The app uses the project’s Rust rules engine and a frozen neural checkpoint. Metal runs inference on your Mac’s GPU; CPU also works. Search is additional planning around that same network. The bot learns nothing from this game, and the app does not send your moves to a cloud service.

Undo turn takes back your most recent move and the bot’s response. Refreshing the page keeps the game while this server is running. Restarting the server starts a fresh board. Save game downloads a local JSON record of the moves.

Keyboard users can move around the board with arrow keys and place with Enter or Space. Piece selection, transforms and settings are ordinary buttons and controls.
